Trío VibrArt

The trio

Three
Musicians

Founded in Berlin in 2015, the Trío VibrArt brings together three outstanding individual careers, interwoven to give voice to the piano trio repertoire with commitment, integrity and authenticity. With Juan Pérez Floristán at the piano, Miguel Colom on violin and Fernando Arias on cello, the ensemble took shape in Berlin after many years of friendship and a shared passion for music. Its name, “VibrArt”, highlights the power of chamber music to convey, to communicate and to make its listeners vibrate.

The three members have led – and continue to lead – parallel musical lives: they trained at the Escuela Reina Sofía in Madrid and in Berlin, and today combine their chamber music activity with solo, teaching and orchestral projects.

Each of them has a distinguished individual career. Juan Pérez Floristán is a winner of the Santander and Arthur Rubinstein competitions and teaches at the Barenboim-Said Akademie; Miguel Colom is concertmaster of the Spanish National Orchestra and teaches at the Escuela Reina Sofía and the Barenboim-Said Akademie; and Fernando Arias is a professor at the Conservatorio Superior de Música de Madrid, teaches at the Escuela Reina Sofía and has won numerous international prizes.

The trio has received guidance from Eberhard Feltz, Menahem Pressler and Kennedy Moretti, among others. Its repertoire today spans many of the great works for piano trio, from Mozart to Lera Auerbach by way of Beethoven, Brahms, Schubert, Dvořák, Mendelssohn and Shostakovich.

The trio performs regularly throughout Europe, at venues and festivals such as the Pollença Festival, the Marvão Festival in Portugal, Schloss Elmau, the Salle Cortot in Paris, the Fundación Juan March in Madrid and the Otoño Musical Soriano. It has also appeared as soloist trio with orchestras including the Spanish National Orchestra, the Barcelona Symphony and Catalonia National Orchestra, the Royal Seville Symphony Orchestra and the Orquesta de la Diputación de Alicante, in halls such as the Auditorio Nacional de Música in Madrid, L’Auditori in Barcelona and the Teatro de la Maestranza in Seville.
